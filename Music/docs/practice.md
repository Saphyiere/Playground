# Practice and Play

The owner's design, 12 November, put together from the four reviews and the mock-ups on the canvas
([Prima: Practise and Play through](https://claude.ai/artifact/BDoxbjCTPokP5ZWP1ZkfAo)), with the owner's answers to its
questions the same day, and built the same week. Every screen there is used, and how much the app says while you practise is
yours to set. Where the owner didn't say, my picks are marked *(pick)*. [concept.md](concept.md) describes it with the rest of
the piece's page.

## The piece's front: Practice or Play

- A notation piece opens on its music, with About this piece under it (what's been done on it), and two big keys on its bar:
  **Practice** and **Play**. Nothing else is on the bar but the piece's level. The music scrolls, and a tap on it does
  nothing there. A PDF whose notes haven't been read opens on its pages, as before.
- Both are taps on the screen. The piano's buttons (the low C♯ and D♯) never press them, nor Finish practice, Keep
  practising, Back to practising, In-depth review or Done: starting and finishing are always deliberate. Inside Practice and
  Play the piano's go key still starts a run, and presses Again, since your hands are on the keys by then.

## Practice

- **Practice** starts a practice session on the piece, and the page is the piece with all its tools: loops, bars to start
  from, hands, In time and Wait for me, the tempo, Listen, Options, full screen. You start each run as before.
- **Two clocks** beside the piece's name (in portrait, on the bar beside it): **Playing**, the time a run or Listen is going
  or you're playing the piano yourself, the music stopped too (going over a bar on your own counts: a key within the last
  ten seconds *(pick)*); and **Idle**, the rest of the session: looking at the music, working out a fingering, a drink of
  water.
- **Finish practice**, beside them, ends the session and shows the look back. Pressed while a run plays, the run stops and
  is kept with the session first.
- **After 15 minutes with nothing played** the session pauses: the page is covered by "Still practising?" with **Keep
  practising** and **Finish practice**, both taps; a key played on the piano carries on too *(pick)*. Leaving the piece, or
  the tablet sleeping or Prima put away, pauses it the same way, and coming back shows the cover. The clocks stop while it's
  paused, and Finish practice from the cover ends the session as of the last thing played. A session left paused until
  another day has been finished that way, and the piece opens on its front *(pick)*.
- Today and You count a piece's practice minutes from its sessions' playing time, added as it's counted (every half minute,
  and as the session pauses or finishes), and from Play's runs. A run in Practice doesn't add its own minutes on top.

## How much Practice tells you

In Settings › Practice, and on the piece's Options for a quick change. Three ways, each a set of the four settings under it,
which Settings lets you change one by one (the piece's Options have the ways and whether a loop waits):

| | Simple | Standard | Hands-on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| After a run | nothing: it's ready to go again | its summary under the music | its review takes the screen |
| Round a loop, each pass's figure | no: the bar counts the passes only | in the bar | in the bar |
| A loop, after each pass | goes straight on | goes straight on | waits for you |
| At a run's end | waits for you | waits for you | waits for you |

- **Simple** is for learning: loops, In time, Wait for me, hands, everything but the reviews. Nothing tells you how it went.
  The marks stay on the music after a run if Marks as you play is on; with it off, nothing marks the music at all *(pick)*.
- **Standard** is the app as it was.
- **Hands-on** gives each run the in-depth review, for that one run, with **Again** (the run again from the same bar; the
  go key presses it) and **Back to practising**, which goes back with all of the run's marks on the music.
- **Round a loop** is a setting of its own in every way: **Go straight on**, to drill a riff over and over, or **Wait after
  each pass**, for a breather to look at what happened. Waiting, each pass is a run of its own, ended and kept as one (in
  Hands-on, its review takes the screen), and the next starts with the loop's key, the go key or Again; the loop still counts
  on, its pass and how the last went in the bar and every pass so far in the summary (in Hands-on, at the review's head), while
  it's the same loop, hand and mode (round 2's reviews).
- **At a run's end** is too: **Wait for me**, or **Go again**, where a run that comes to its end starts over after its
  count-in, until you stop it. Every way starts on waiting.
- **The marks on the music** (while you play, and afterwards) and **the cursor** stay settings of their own, as before.
- Whatever you choose, every run and every pass is kept, with what went wrong in each bar and the marks it left, so the look
  back and the in-depth review have them all.

## The look back (Finish practice)

- As on the canvas, taking the page: the session's playing and idle time, its stretches, passes and runs through; each
  stretch you practised (its bars, the hand and the mode) with its runs, or round a loop its passes, as a line, the best
  named and its first and last figures beside it, and its tempo (a step on the line where it changed); **Still hard**, the bar
  with most to fix in the last third of the times it was played (a wrong or missed note weighing three times anything else),
  and **Fixed**, the bar that was worst at first and clean at the end; and how the stretches played both times compare with
  the session before on the piece that had any (**Earlier today**, **Since yesterday**…): a faster tempo, or else the best
  figure. Still hard used to count the runs a bar went wrong in, which named a bar with one note let go early every time over
  a bar that was a mess and got fixed; the reviews of round 2 found the look back and the review naming different bars.
- Keys: **Keep practising** (the session goes on), **In-depth review** beside it, **Practise bar N** (the session carried on,
  looping the bar still hard with a bar either side), and **Done** (back to the piece's front).
- No "next time" line: it suggests, and the instructor work is on hold.

## The in-depth review

- The session as a whole, taking the screen as on the canvas: its figures, led by where it ended (its last stretch, first →
  last, and that stretch's tempo), not the session's average; its lines (notes, timing, held, Look at and Fixed, by the look
  back's rule) over every run and pass, slips put right and Wait for me's chord notes not together named apart from wrong notes;
  the bars shaded by what went wrong against the worst bar; and the music of the bar you tap, outlined, marked as the latest
  run that played it left it. It opens on the bar still hard. **Back** returns to the look back, **Practise bar N** loops the
  bar picked, **Done** goes to the front.
- **A long piece**: up to 24 bars every bar has its cell. Past that, only the bars that had something wrong, the most to fix
  first, up to 16, each with its number, and over them a thin strip of the whole piece with each of them marked on it, so
  you can see where they are. More than 16 with something wrong: the 16 with the most, and "and 9 more" to show them all.
- Hands-on's run and Play's run open it too, for their one run.

## Play

- **Play** opens the piece full screen, the page and nothing else: a slim strip at the top with the piece's name, the tempo
  (tap it to change it), **Start** and **Done**, so the tempo can be set first. Start, or the piano's go key, counts in; the
  strip then says the bar and the time, with Stop; at the end it keeps only the tempo, the quick review having the keys.
- No cursor and no marks while it plays, whatever the settings say. It's In time, with the click, from the first bar to the
  end, both hands, whatever bar and hand Practice had. Following you without a click is the advanced reviewer's ask, on hold
  with the other seats.
- **At the end** (or Stop): every note marked on the music, and a very quick review at the bottom: the headline and a few
  words, like "93% right in time · 88 of 95 notes · look at bar 1", with **Again**, **Practise bar 1** (Practice started,
  looping that bar with a bar either side), **In-depth review** and **Done**.
- A play-through is kept as a run, and counts in About's "played through" and its best.

## How it's built

- **The session** is a row of its own (the piece, when it started and finished, its playing and idle time, whether it's
  paused and when something was last played, and the way it was practised), kept every half minute. Its clock is
  `PracticeClock` in Prima.Core, tested with the rest of the core.
- **Each run** keeps the session it was played in, or that it was Play's; what went wrong in each bar, for the run and for
  each pass round a loop; and the marks it left on the music. `SessionReview` in Prima.Core sums a session's runs up into its
  stretches, the bar still hard and each bar's trouble, for the look back and the in-depth review.
- **The piece's page** has five states: the front, Practice (the page as it was, with the clocks and Finish practice, and the
  pause's cover over it), the look back, the in-depth review, and Play (full screen, with its strip).
- Checked in the preview, both looks, the tablet's landscape and portrait: the front, a session's runs and loops, Finish
  practice mid-loop, the look back and the in-depth review, Play and its quick review, leaving and coming back to the cover,
  and the three ways, Wait after each pass and Go again.

## Left for the tablet

Only the tablet and the piano can show these:

- the clocks counting keys played with the music stopped as playing time, and a pause after 15 minutes with nothing played;
- the cover after the tablet sleeps or Prima is put away, and a key on the piano carrying the session on;
- the go key starting runs, loops' next passes and Again, and never Practice, Play, Finish practice or Done;
- Play's click and its quick review after a real play-through.

## Decided

1. Three ways: Simple, Standard and Hands-on.
2. A loop after each pass: a setting, waiting for you or going straight on; Hands-on starts on waiting, the others on going
   straight on.
3. Play: a Start key first, so the tempo can be set.
4. Two clocks, Playing and Idle, in place of one that pauses after five idle minutes.
5. After 15 minutes with nothing played, the session pauses under a cover asking whether you're still practising.
