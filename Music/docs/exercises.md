## In short

Prima's next technique exercises should be chord work, built in this order: **solid triads walked and inverted with rests, prepared chord jumps, cadences played near and far, and a bass-and-chord left hand**; then cheap overlays on the scale drill it already has (contrary motion, chromatic, staccato, loud and soft, one hand unlike the other), then pedal changes and voicing. Chords are the one family that RCM, the adult methods and online teachers all place at grades 1 to 5 and that Prima, following ABRSM's lists, lacks: RCM examines solid and broken triads with inversions from its first level, adult methods teach primary chords before arpeggios, and the exercise books for this level hold no leap or chord-change drills to copy, so generated ones fill a real hole. MIDI judges these well at the keys (the first landing, how far a miss missed, how tightly a chord's notes arrive, travel time, loudness) and not at all above them: fingering, the eyes and the hand's path stay invisible. The evidence for any particular exercise is thin and is labelled as such throughout: **no controlled trial shows that Hanon or any other drill improves playing**, and the one training study of a piano leap found that 160 repetitions in half an hour changed nothing. What research does support is a way of practising: short, quiet, mostly correct runs with rests, tempo tied to accuracy, patterns mixed once learned, and progress judged on a later day. Twenty minutes a day is far inside every injury threshold; the hazards are force and wide spans, so octaves, stretches and loud, fast loops stay out. **YouTube tutorials cannot be scraped, nor their transcripts pulled, under YouTube's terms, even for a private app**, and there is no need: exercise patterns are not copyrightable, so the owner watches or reads, the idea is written down in fresh words, and Prima's generator writes its own notes. Every scoring band below is a starting value to tune on the owner's own runs, and a handful of facts about the P-225's MIDI output still have to be checked on the instrument.

## Chords are the grade 1 to 5 family Prima lacks

### Two dozen headings, and the boards examine a third of them

The exercise literature and the exam boards carve technique into the same two dozen headings. Joseffy's 1902 school gives a chapter each to five-finger exercises, thumb passage, scales, trills, arpeggios, thirds, sixths, octaves, repetitions, chords, skips, stretches, touch and soft playing ([Joseffy, contents](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)); Beringer adds crossing hands and "playing different rhythms with both hands together" ([Alfred listing](https://www.alfred.com/products/beringer-daily-technical-studies-for-piano-00-pk-0002730)); Trinity sorts its composed exam exercises under "tone, balance and voicing", "coordination" and "finger & wrist strength and flexibility" ([Trinity syllabus](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)); and RCM groups its tests as scales, chords and arpeggios ([RCM syllabus](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)).

At grades 1 to 5 the boards examine only the first third of that list, and they disagree about the rest. **ABRSM asks for scales, contrary-motion scales, chromatic scales and arpeggios, and nothing else.** Its 2021 revision dropped broken chords ([Pianodao](https://pianodao.com/2020/07/12/abrsm-piano-scales-2021/)); staccato scales and a diminished seventh arrive only at Grade 5; the section carries 21 of 150 marks; and the 2027 & 2028 syllabus leaves the requirements unchanged ([ABRSM syllabus](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf), [Pianodao 2026](https://pianodao.com/2026/06/04/abrsm-piano-syllabus-2027-28/)). Trinity asks for fewer keys but wants scales *f* or *p* from Grade 2 and staccato from Grade 4, and adds three short composed exercises a grade ([Trinity syllabus](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)); AMEB also sets three technical exercises per grade ([TopMusic](https://topmusic.co/the-new-ameb-series-18-piano-syllabus-frequently-asked-questions/)), though its key lists were not obtained. **RCM is the fullest: tonic triads, broken and solid, in root position and inversions from Level 1**, the solid chords at crotchet = 100, then 112, then 120 by Levels 3 and 4, hands together from Level 4, and a I–V–I cadence closing the pattern from Level 5 ([RCM syllabus](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf), [RCM charts](https://colorinmypiano.com/wp-content/files/RCM_2022_Technical_Requirements_Charts_Prep-10.pdf)).

Adult method books side with RCM. Faber's adult course gives every unit a "3-Minute Technique" page and moves from seconds and thirds for each hand alone, through parallel and contrary motion and five-finger positions in new keys, to its first one-octave scale in Unit 12, and then at once to the G7 chord, primary chords and a chord étude; its second book adds pedalling, triads and their inversions, an Alberti bass study and, almost last, one-octave arpeggios in Unit 15 ([Faber Book 1](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1302/), [Faber Book 2](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1334/)). ABRSM asks for arpeggios at Grade 1 and never for solid chords or cadences. Prima's technique drills follow the exams' scale and arpeggio lists, so the app has inherited ABRSM's blind spot: **a learner who follows ABRSM alone misses the chord work that RCM and the adult methods treat as basic.**

### Pattern books, graded études and one warm-up series

The canon comes in three kinds. Pattern books transpose a small cell: each of Hanon's first twenty exercises is one eight-note figure that starts on C and repeats a white key higher every bar across two octaves, then reverses ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_in_60_Exercises), [Mutopia source](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/HanonCL/virtuoso-pianist-pt1/)); Schmitt's Op. 16 opens with 213 one-position exercises, each to be repeated ten or twenty times and then transposed with the same fingering ([Schirmer scan](https://archive.org/details/preparatoryexerc16schm)); Pischna prints every exercise in every key ([Alfred edition](https://www.popplersmusic.com/item/00-637/)). Graded études wrap one figure in a short piece: Czerny's own title makes Op. 849 the "introduction to" the School of Velocity ([IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/30_%C3%89tudes_de_m%C3%A9canisme,_Op.849_%28Czerny,_Carl%29)), and Burgmüller's Op. 100 runs from about ABRSM Grade 2 to about Grade 5 ([Practising the Piano Online Academy](https://www.informance.biz/online-academy/page/132-introduction)). Burnam's *A Dozen a Day* is the daily warm-up series for children ([PTNA](https://enc.piano.or.jp/en/persons/6835)). Hanon says his own book "may be taken up after the pupil has studied about a year" ([Schirmer Hanon](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)). Read against the books' titles and the boards' placements, only Hanon's Parts I and II, Schmitt, Czerny's Op. 599, 821 and 849, Duvernoy, Burgmüller's Op. 100, the Little Pischna and Burnam sit in this learner's band; the rest of the shelf, from Czerny's Op. 299 (an RCM Level 8 étude) to Cramer, Clementi, Brahms, Liszt, Cortot and Dohnányi, is for advanced players ([RCM syllabus](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)). That banding is this research's synthesis; no published table equates the collections with grades.

The books in this learner's band do not train what the owner named. Hanon's sixty exercises cover finger patterns, scales, arpeggios, repeated notes, double notes, octaves and tremolo, and list nothing for leaps or chord changes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_in_60_Exercises)); Joseffy has a chapter on skips, but in a school of advanced playing ([Joseffy, contents](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)). At this level leap practice comes from pieces (Estrin's example is the Musette from the Anna Magdalena Notebook; Burgmüller's "La Styrienne", marked crotchet = 176, has right-hand leaps as its hard places) and from RCM's triad lists ([Estrin](https://www.pianistmagazine.com/blogs/how-to-play-big-leaps-successfully-on-the-piano), [PTNA](https://enc.piano.or.jp/en/musics/23989)). **There is no public-domain "Hanon for leaps" to transcribe, so chord jumps have to be generated.**

One design point follows from how the books specify their material. A generator needs four ingredients to cover almost every heading: a short cell of scale degrees with its fingering, a transposition rule (up the scale each bar, as Hanon does, or through the keys, as Pischna does), a mirror rule for the left hand, and an overlay of touch, dynamics or rhythm. That is an inference from the sources, and it describes what Prima's scale, arpeggio and five-finger generator already does; chords need one more ingredient, a shape and where it moves.

## No trial backs any exercise, so method carries the weight

### Dexterity is control, not strength, and Hanon is untested

This research found no controlled study, at any level, of whether Hanon-type exercises improve playing or carry over to pieces. The indirect laboratory evidence is consistent, though most of it is observational and from conservatory-trained pianists. Pianists tap faster and more evenly than non-musicians, most of all with the ring and little fingers, yet their pinch and grasp force is the same: "piano training... changed the ability to move individual fingers rapidly, but not their flexor strength" ([Aoki et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1123/mcj.9.1.23)). For the same tempo and loudness, amateurs press harder and hold longer than experts, who use less co-contraction and move the key from the shoulder and forearm ([Parlitz et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0021-9290%2898%2900113-4), [Furuya & Kinoshita 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-007-1184-9)). Ten adults new to music reduced the unwanted co-movement between their fingers in four days of ordinary piano practice, without isolation drills ([Furuya et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2014.06.031)), and in 19 expert pianists followed for about 27 months, the evenness of scales tracked total practice time, not what they reported doing with scales ([Jabusch et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.humov.2008.08.001)).

Teachers split along the same line. Chang, Huang and a teacher writing in *Pianist* reject strength-building and finger-lifting drills while accepting scales and arpeggios, and the Taubman school rejects exercise routines altogether ([Chang](https://fundamentals-of-piano-practice.readthedocs.io/chapter1/ch1_topics/III.7.html), [Huang](https://www.pianoeducation.org/pnotmi2.html), [Rizzo](https://www.pianistmagazine.com/blogs/this-is-why-hanon-exercises-are-a-waste-of-time-and-possibly-dangerous/), [Golandsky](https://crosseyedpianist.com/tag/edna-golandsky/)). What stays disputed is whether isolated finger-independence drills have any place: Chang keeps a light one, with the warning "do not press down firmly as this is a form of stress", while Whiteside rejected finger technique altogether ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abby_Whiteside)). Also disputed is whether Hanon helps beginners, as Huang holds, or harms them, as Taubman held; a 2026 peer-reviewed reading notes that her claim that curled fingers lead to dystonia has "not been scientifically validated" ([Sabo et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.70760/KFXI1598)).

Which patterns to drill still matters, for a reason one controlled study supplies. The least experienced child pianists carried learning over only to sequences with the same fingers and the same notes; skilled adults transferred whenever the musical structure stayed the same ([Palmer & Meyer 2000](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.00216)). At this learner's level an exercise pays back mainly in the pieces that share its patterns. That is the case for building exercises from the textures and keys of the music being learned and against importing a drill book whole.

### Mostly correct, short, spaced and mixed

The best-supported finding is that how something is practised matters more than how long. Seventeen advanced pianists practised a hard three-bar passage for as long as they chose; their rank the next day was unrelated to practice time or the number of trials and tracked the **share of trials played correctly (r = −.51 over all trials, −.71 over complete ones)** ([Duke, Simmons & Cash 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429408328851)). It is one observational study, and it sets the first rule: keep most runs correct, and after a flawed run make the next one slower or aimed at the spot.

Scheduling evidence is controlled but small. Accuracy on a keyboard sequence improved in a second session only when 24 hours separated the sessions ([Simmons 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429411424798)), while gaps of up to 15 minutes inside one sitting did nothing ([Wiseheart et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5553926/)); whether sleep adds learning or only protects it is disputed by a meta-analysis of 34 studies ([Pan & Rickard 2015](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000009)). Twenty-five pianists played melodies practised in a fixed order faster on the day and those practised in random order faster two days later, while believing fixed order had worked better ([Abushanab & Bishara 2013](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-013-0311-z)); meta-analyses of general motor learning rate the benefit of mixed order as medium, larger in adults and nearly gone outside the laboratory ([Brady 2004](https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.99.1.116-126), [Czyż et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-65753-3)). Practising a second, similar melody straight after a new one suppressed the first one's overnight gain in 60 musicians ([Allen 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0305735612463947)). Short rests lift performance within a sitting, but the latest experiments find that the advantage "vanished within seconds after training" ([Das et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2509233122)); rests stay in the design for the safety reasons below, not as a learning aid.

Three things teachers treat as settled are not. No controlled experiment compares slow practice with practice at tempo at the piano: the support is a survey in which 96.5% of 256 instrumentalists use it ([Allingham & Wöllner 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/03057356211073481)), set against one experiment in which a passage learned at one tempo was played less accurately at another ([Duke & Pierce 1991](https://doi.org/10.2307/3344689)) and one, with wind players, in which steady, at-tempo and alternating tempo patterns made no difference ([Henley 1999](https://doi.org/10.31390/gradschool_disstheses.6992)). No study says how many correct repetitions are worth doing; "three" and "five in a row" are teaching conventions ([Gebrian](https://www.mollygebrian.com/s/Gebrian-Appendix-A.pdf)). And rhythm variations, in the one study of 27 pianists, lowered hand-muscle activity without adding speed ([Furuya & Yokota 2018](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00922.2017)). Two cheap aids do have controlled support: hearing the target ten times before practising enlarged gains both during practice and overnight ([Cash et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429413520409)), and for complete novices two tempi in a fixed order gave smoother movement on new tempi than six tempi or a shuffled order, on an immediate test only ([Caramiaux et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5832267/)).

Feedback after the run helps when it says what was wrong and by how much: among 228 learners, error-size or graded feedback produced learning and pass/fail feedback produced none ([St. Germain et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-022-02170-5)), and a meta-analysis of 61 papers found no benefit from rationing feedback ([McKay et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/v2cp7)). Feedback shown during the movement is the risky kind: it builds dependence in simple tasks, and in conservatory percussionists it was "potentially disruptive to timing processes" ([Sigrist et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-012-0333-8), [Brandmeyer et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-010-0291-6)); no piano study exists. Cues about sound and keys instead of fingers helped in three small music studies, two of them at the piano ([Duke, Cash & Allen 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429410396093)), yet a bias-corrected re-analysis of the wider literature puts the effect near zero ([McKay et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.51224/srxiv.304)); the cue costs nothing, so it stays the default. Letting learners set their own schedule shows no learning benefit once selective reporting is corrected ([McKay et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/8d3nb)), and learners prefer the blocked repetition that works less well, so the app sets the order.

Adults do learn piano motor skills: a year of lessons improved fine motor control in 156 older novices against music listening ([Worschech et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.16031)). In people over 60, though, what is learned in a session is kept less reliably overnight and is more easily disrupted by what follows it ([Brown et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0006683), [Roig et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neurobiolaging.2014.02.022)). That evidence is from laboratory key-press tasks and says nothing about learners between 30 and 60, so it argues for a direction, not a number: fewer new patterns per sitting, and a later day as the test.

### Twenty minutes is safe; force and span are the hazards

Playing-related pain is common among serious pianists, reported by 26% to 93% across studies in one systematic review ([Bragge et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/occmed/kqi177)), but the studies are cross-sectional surveys of students and professionals and no risk factor is firmly established. The doses that recur are far above this learner's: more than four hours a day ([Furuya et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.21091/mppa.2006.3023)), more than 20 hours a week or 60 minutes without a break ([Amaral Corrêa et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pmrj.2018.04.001)). What bears on exercise design is how load is applied. Of 70 pianists whose overuse pain began while they were practising a particular technique, **74% were practising octaves or chords** that spread the thumb and little finger wide ([Sakai 2002](https://doi.org/10.21091/mppa.2002.4028)); smaller hands recur as a factor ([Sakai & Shimawaki 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/1753193409352405)); accepting "no pain, no gain" stayed an independent risk in one survey ([Bruno et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00420-007-0279-8)); and clinicians list sudden increases in playing load as a modifiable cause ([Chan & Ackermann 2014](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00706)). In the laboratory, looping two bars of Hanon No. 7 loudly at crotchet = 112 brought 30 of 49 trained pianists to high exertion in about three and a half minutes ([Goubault et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8047012/)), and a fast tempo roughly doubled forearm muscle activity in untrained adults ([Chong et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4554952/)).

Advice on warm-up and breaks is expert consensus, not tested prevention. A systematic review found no studies of upper-body warm-up and injury ([McCrary et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2014-094228)); fifteen minutes of warm-up changed only perceived exertion in 55 violinists ([McCrary et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000765)); BAPAM's guidance for musicians comes down to regular breaks, something soft and slow to finish, and "STOP if you feel pain" ([BAPAM](https://www.bapam.org.uk/dont-cramp-your-style/)). Musician's dystonia follows at least ten years of intensive playing in professionals ([Rozanski et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.3238/arztebl.2015.0871)) and is not a realistic risk at this dose. No study of pain or injury in adult beginners was found, so the rules that follow are extrapolations from students and professionals: quiet dynamics; nothing held at full span; no octave or wide-chord drills; runs of seconds with rests between them; a slow, soft first exercise; a stop on pain with no reward for pushing through; and load raised gradually. Prima's Finger independence drill, its held-note figures above all, sits on the disputed side of this evidence and should stay short and light instead of growing.

## Teachers repeat ten families; YouTube is for watching

### What online teachers teach, read from their own written posts

No videos were watched for this research; the teachers' exercises were read from their own written companions to their lessons, in prose, since notation printed as images could not be read. Ten families recur across at least three independent sources: five-finger patterns, scales on a metronome ladder, arpeggios, a triad shape moved through the keys and chord types cycled through all twelve roots, cadences and four-chord progressions in every key, left-hand accompaniment figures, two-hand independence drills laid on a scale, held-note finger independence, speed drills, and, with less written detail, octaves and leaps. The chord families are the ones Prima lacks. Pianote walks a 1-3-5 triad shape up the white keys, solid and then broken, and builds a four-chord loop in six layers with inversions chosen so that the hand barely moves ([Pianote, 4 exercises](https://www.pianote.com/blog/beginner-exercises/), [Pianote, warm-ups](https://www.pianote.com/blog/easy-piano-warm-ups/)); Piano With Jonny cycles six triad types on each of twelve roots in about five minutes a day ([Piano With Jonny](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/the-top-exercise-to-practice-piano-chords/)); tonebase's Nicole DiPaolo rotates the cadence I–IV–I–V–I through every key in a month ([tonebase](https://www.tonebase.co/piano-blog-posts/piano-practice)). The independence drills are overlays, not new notes: one hand loud and the other soft, one legato and the other staccato, one in quarters and the other in eighths, on a scale or five-finger pattern ([PianoTV](https://www.pianotv.net/2016/10/piano-exercises-hand-independence/), [Pianote, 4 exercises](https://www.pianote.com/blog/beginner-exercises/)).

Where teachers give numbers they cluster at a slow anchor and small doses. Estrin sets the metronome at 60 and plays one, then two, then four notes to the beat, moving on after four clean repetitions, for 5 to 10 minutes a day ([Living Pianos, scales](https://www.livingpianos.com/videos/how-to-practice-scales-and-arpeggios-on-the-piano/), [Living Pianos, 10 minutes](https://www.livingpianos.com/articles/10-minutes-that-will-transform-your-piano-playing/)). Fitch keeps one pulse of about 60 while a scale grows from one octave in quarter notes to four in sixteenths, and holds that "Two or three repetitions with the full involvement of the mind and the ear will usually suffice" ([Fitch, scales](https://practisingthepiano.com/a-beautiful-process-for-scales/), [Fitch, exercises](https://practisingthepiano.com/on-technical-exercises/)). Piano With Jonny caps technique at about 15% of practice, which is three minutes of twenty ([Piano With Jonny](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/5-essential-piano-exercises/)), and Pianote's 2025 routine warms up for 3 to 5 minutes on the first four chords of the song being learned, in place of Hanon ([Pianote, routine](https://www.pianote.com/blog/piano-practice-routine/)).

The live disagreements are about manner. Estrin recommends Hanon daily at a fairly strong volume with raised, rounded fingers ([Living Pianos, Hanon](https://www.livingpianos.com/articles/learning-hanon-part-1-the-first-exercises-the-virtuoso-pianist/)); Fitch uses the same patterns only as a "blank canvas" for wrist and thumb movement and rejects finger-strengthening and high lifting ([Fitch, Hanon](https://practisingthepiano.com/jailbreaking-hanon-piano-exercises/)); DiPaolo does not use Hanon at all, requires only scales, arpeggios and cadences, and makes other studies out of the student's own hard passages ([tonebase](https://www.tonebase.co/piano-blog-posts/piano-practice)). Set against the force and fatigue evidence above, Fitch's and DiPaolo's side is the one to follow. Because teachers also disagree about finger height and the thumb, Prima's cue text should keep to what nobody disputes and MIDI can check: evenness, hands together, balance between the hands, clean releases. Several teachers the owner might follow could not be covered from written sources (Bill Hilton, Heart of the Keys, Jazer Lee, Nahre Sol's videos, Josh Wright's twelve scale methods).

### Apps grade pitch and timing and stop there

Commercial apps score two things. Piano Marvel "primarily assesses note accuracy and rhythm accuracy", leaves out dynamics and articulation, and publishes its bands of 80, 90, 96 and 100% ([Piano Marvel](https://pianomarvel.com/feature/play-mode)); Melodics measures how closely on time each note was, steps the tempo up 10 BPM after a clean pass and offers a wait mode ([Melodics, scoring](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/15092017-lesson-scoring), [Melodics, practice](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/6777027-practice-mode)); an independent review says Skoove "grades pitch and timing, and stops there" ([Pianoers](https://pianoers.com/skoove-review/)). No vendor checked publishes a timing window in milliseconds, and a 2020 review of the assessment literature judged commercial tutors "not reliable enough to be used in educational settings" ([Lerch et al. 2020](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.53)). Technique content is thin in the song-led apps; the nearest thing to what Prima needs is a small app, Scale Study, that "records the fastest tempo you can play it cleanly" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758663846)). That record, kept per item and hand, is the one borrowing worth making. The opening is the larger point: nobody scores evenness, balance, releases or chord spread, which are what teachers tell students to listen for and what MIDI measures.

### Scraping is barred; the ideas are free

YouTube's terms (the United States version, effective 15 December 2023; the project's notes place the owner in a US time zone) allow viewing "for your personal, non-commercial use" and showing videos through YouTube's embeddable player. They forbid users to "access, reproduce, download" content except as the service authorises, and to access the service "using any automated means (such as robots, botnets or scrapers)" without written permission ([YouTube Terms of Service](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms)). The personal-use phrase is the scope of the viewing permission, not an exemption from the rest: YouTube's help pages restate that the terms prohibit "unauthorized downloads and scraping" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15509945?hl=en)). The developer rules close the other door. API clients must not store audiovisual content, scrape, or "use any technology other than YouTube API Services to access or retrieve API Data" ([Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies)), and the official captions endpoint hands caption text only to someone with "permission to edit the video" ([captions.download](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/captions/download)). **A downloader, a transcript library or a script that fetches watch pages is therefore outside the terms whether or not Prima is ever published.** This is a reading of the terms, not legal advice; whether stream-ripping also breaches US anti-circumvention law was still before an appeals court in April 2026 ([TorrentFreak](https://torrentfreak.com/yout-com-hopes-supreme-courts-cox-ruling-helps-its-case-riaa-disagrees/)).

Copyright points the other way, and that makes the owner's plan workable. US law excludes "any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation" from protection ([Copyright Office, Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)). The Copyright Office names "Diatonic or chromatic scales", "Arpeggios" and "Chord symbols based on standard chord progressions" as common property, and describes method books as common material plus "original elements, such as instructional text, new music, and original musical exercises" ([Compendium, ch. 800](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap800/ch800-performing-arts.pdf)). Courts have refused protection to a fixed sequence of 26 yoga poses as a "system" and to a chord progression paired with a harmonic rhythm ([Bikram](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-9th-circuit/1715117.html), [Sheeran](https://copyrightlately.com/ed-sheeran-wins-copyright-appeal-5-things-to-know/)). The line runs between the idea (a triad shape walked up the white keys, a cadence through every key, one hand staccato against the other legato), which anyone may implement, and a teacher's expression: the wording, the video and its captions, an edited page with its fingering, or an exercise that is real new music. No case or Office statement deals with a piano technique exercise as such, so where a stock pattern ends and an original study begins is a matter of degree.

The permitted route follows directly. The owner watches a tutorial, or Claude reads the teacher's public written post; the idea is written as a short specification in fresh words (pattern, hands, keys, tempo ladder, length, what to listen for); Prima's generator writes its own notes from that and gives the exercise its own name; and a link, or YouTube's own embedded player at 200 by 200 pixels or more with nothing laid over it, points back to the lesson ([Required Minimum Functionality](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality)). Where concrete historical exercises are wanted, the old books are the clean source. Everything published in 1930 or earlier is in the US public domain as of 1 January 2026 ([Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain](https://cspd.law.duke.edu/publicdomainday/2026/)): Hanon (1873), Schmitt, Czerny, Burgmüller, Pischna, Joseffy, and even Cortot (1928) and Dohnányi (1929) ([IMSLP, Hanon](https://imslp.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_%28Hanon,_Charles-Louis%29), [IMSLP, Cortot](https://imslp.org/wiki/Rational_Principles_of_Piano_Technique_%28Cortot,_Alfred%29), [IMSLP, Dohnányi](https://imslp.org/wiki/Essential_Finger_Exercises_%28Dohn%C3%A1nyi,_Ern%C5%91%29)). Burnam's *A Dozen a Day* (1950; its renewal was not checked, so it is treated as in copyright) and the boards' and method books' modern exercises are not, and serve as categories only ([Internet Archive record](https://archive.org/details/dozendaytechnica0000burn)). In life-plus-70 countries Philipp, Dohnányi and Cortot stay protected until the end of 2028, 2030 and 2032 by this research's arithmetic ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/copyright/how-long-copyright-lasts)), and a modern edition's fingering and commentary can carry rights of its own.

## MIDI judges every landing, not the hand behind it

### What the key data can carry

Prima receives press time, release time, press velocity and the pedals. Timing is good enough for everything proposed here. USB MIDI interfaces measured on a PC averaged about 1 ms of latency with about 1 ms of spread and a range of 0 to 4 ms ([Schultz 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-018-1042-7)), and Android stamps each message when the USB read returns, once per read, not when the key moved ([Android source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/master/services/usb/java/com/android/server/usb/UsbDirectMidiDevice.java)). No measurement of Android's own input jitter was found, so differences under about 3 ms should be treated as noise and thresholds of 10 ms or more as safe; constant delays cancel out of every within-run measure. Velocity is a number from 1 to 127 whose meaning is the instrument's own: measured synthesizers differed by more than 60 dB in dynamic range ([Dannenberg 2006](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rbd/papers/velocity-icmc2006.pdf)). Loudness rules therefore belong on the player's own range, which Prima's "Your soft and loud" already records. On an acoustic grand a key takes about 25 ms to reach the bottom at forte and about 160 ms at piano, so the louder note of a chord registers earlier for mechanical reasons ([Goebl 2001](https://iwk.mdw.ac.at/goebl/papers/Goebl_JASA2001_melodyLead.pdf)). Fingering is out of reach: two pianists finger the same piece alike on only 60 to 80% of notes, and the best models match a pianist on about 60 to 64% ([Nakamura et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10237)). The eyes, the hand's path, tension and a silent arrival on the keys produce no data at all.

Several facts about the P-225 itself are unverified, because Yamaha's MIDI reference could not be opened: whether note-off velocity varies, what the pedal sends, whether the touch setting changes the velocities sent, how fast the action repeats, and how the notes of one chord are stamped. Users report a fixed note-off velocity of 64 on some other Yamaha models ([Modartt forum](https://forum.modartt.com/viewtopic.php?pid=995116)), so the first of these cannot be assumed. Prima's Keyboard check can read all of them off the instrument.

Reference values exist for experts and almost none for learners. Professionals' scale unevenness is 8 to 9 ms at eight notes a second and becomes audible at about 10 ms, or 8% of the gap between notes ([van Vugt et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00134)); conservatory students' velocity varies by about 6 units within a run ([van Vugt, Treutler et al. 2013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3705811/)); the two hands of long-term amateurs spread about 14 ms apart against 8 ms in experts, the unit being inferred ([Kim et al. 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0250299)); professionals' chord asynchronies peak below 20 ms ([Hu et al. 2026](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2026/HuEtAl-TISMIR2026.pdf)), and asynchronies within ±30 ms are "not likely to be perceived as such" ([Goebl et al. 2010](https://continuum-hypothesis.com/music/Goebl-InvestigationsBetweenHandSynchronization-2010.pdf)). Teachers rating technique exercises agree within two points on a six-point scale only about 65% of the time, and about 50% for dynamics and legato ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03139)), while for beginners a weighted rate of wrong and extra notes alone correlated 0.86 with the teacher's grade ([Kato et al. 2023](https://eita-nakamura.github.io/articles/Kato_PianoPerformanceAutomaticEvaluation_%20ECTEL2023.pdf)). Prima's present order is therefore the right one for new exercises too: right notes first, then steadiness, then touch, each shown as a measurement beside a reference band and never as a grade.

### Chord changes: first landing, spread and loudness

The chord-change drills come from three patterns the sources already describe. *Same shape, new place* moves a root-position triad up the white keys (C, D minor, E minor, F, G, A minor), a crotchet chord and a crotchet rest at a time, then by thirds, fourths and fifths, then onto triads with a black middle note and onto black–white–black shapes; its first step is RCM's Preparatory triad sequence and Pianote's shape walk ([RCM charts](https://colorinmypiano.com/wp-content/files/RCM_2022_Technical_Requirements_Charts_Prep-10.pdf), [Pianote, 4 exercises](https://www.pianote.com/blog/beginner-exercises/)). The *inversion ladder* is RCM's test for Levels 1 to 4: root position, first inversion, second inversion and root position an octave higher, and back down, hands separately and then together, in the exams' order of keys, against a click that climbs from 80 to RCM's marks ([RCM syllabus](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)); a first-inversion triad takes 1-2-5 in the right hand and a second-inversion triad 5-2-1 in the left ([Piano With Jonny](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/top-piano-chord-inversion-exercise/)). The *cadence loop* takes the progression DiPaolo sets in every key, I–IV–I–V–I ([tonebase](https://www.tonebase.co/piano-blog-posts/piano-practice)), one chord to a beat at crotchet = 60: the right hand first in close position, keeping common notes, from each position of the tonic, then with every chord in root position so that the hand has to jump, over left-hand roots, with the dominant taking its seventh once sevenths are known.

Each landing is scored on five things, all of them this research's proposals built on published anchors. The first is right keys at first landing: the first keys pressed after leaving the previous chord are compared with the target, later corrections do not count, and missing and extra notes are named. Prima's strict slip rule already works this way, and it is what the teachers ask for: Fitch's "golden rule is to hold on to whatever you land on", and Estrin warns that "If you miss the jump repeatedly, you end up reinforcing the mistake" ([Fitch 2019](https://practisingthepiano.com/precision-measurement-in-jumps/), [Estrin](https://www.pianistmagazine.com/blogs/how-to-play-big-leaps-successfully-on-the-piano)). The second is spread, last press minus first press. The two research strands proposed slightly different bands (20, 30 and 50 ms; 30 and 60 ms); the recommendation is to start with **together up to 30 ms, loose from 30 to 50 ms, rolled beyond 50 ms**, draw the professionals' 20 ms as a reference line, and let a deliberately louder top note lead by up to 30 ms. The third is beat error against the click, in Prima's existing bands. The fourth is loudness: the range of velocities inside the chord, and the landing against the chord before it. The fifth is the fastest clean tempo for each key and hand, shown against RCM's mark for the level. Fingering is printed on the drill, since it cannot be checked.

### Chord jumps: prepare, land, hold, then shrink the pause

Prima already owns the two ends of this skill. Its Chords drill tests knowing a shape, and its Keyboard geography drill tests finding single keys without looking, out to leaps of a ninth. What is missing is the travel between shapes, in time.

Almost every source teaches that travel the same way: split the leap into the sideways move and the keystroke, make the move far faster than the music needs so that the hand arrives early and rests on the keys, judge the landing without correcting it, and shrink the pause until the leap is in time ([Fitch 2012](https://practisingthepiano.com/leaps-of-faith-on-practising-waltzes/), [Estrin](https://www.pianistmagazine.com/blogs/how-to-play-big-leaps-successfully-on-the-piano), [Chang](https://fundamentals-of-piano-practice.readthedocs.io/chapter1/ch1_topics/III.7.html#f-practicing-jumps), [Belkin](https://alanbelkinmusic.com/technique/)). Ortmann's 1929 recordings show that preparing and playing in one curve are stages, not rival schools: at two seconds a shift players moved along the keys and then lifted and dropped, and by a third of a second the movement had merged into a single low curve. He also measured that accenting one end of a repeated leap made the movement less accurate, hence his advice of "the lightest possible practice", and he found no downward push-off at any loudness, against Chang's "kick" ([Ortmann 1929](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.89583)). That is one set of old observational recordings plus convergent expert opinion.

The only controlled training study is sobering. Thirty-six music students practised a left-hand leap between two octave grips 15 semitones apart, to be made in 187.5 ms. **After 160 trials in 30 minutes no group made fewer errors, that day or the next**; leaps that took about 280 ms before training stayed well above the goal; and a group that spread its practice over four distances did as well on the target with a quarter of the repetitions ([Bangert et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4128393)). Two lessons follow: one sitting of repetition buys nothing measurable, so gains have to be looked for across days, and distances can be mixed freely. Not looking has the same standing. Better sight-readers glance at the keyboard far less (r = −.77 in 22 pianists) and glance most in the passages that contain jumps ([Cara 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10696908)), and experienced pianists made more wrong notes blindfolded even in a five-finger exercise ([Nunes-Silva et al. 2024](https://citedrive.com/en/discovery/effects-of-altered-sensory-feedback-on-piano-performance-errors-an-exploratory-study), abstract only), but no study shows that practising leaps without looking improves them.

The drill is built in stages from these sources; the stages and numbers are proposals. Material runs from single note to single note with the same finger, to note-to-chord, to chord-to-chord, the order a general exercise set uses ([For Dummies](https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/music/instruments/piano/jump-exercises-for-the-piano-or-keyboard-203706)), and it separates the leap from the change of shape, as Dorn advises ([Dorn](https://pianosage.blogspot.com/2012/08/piano-athletics-leaps-jumps-on-piano.html)): the same triad shape moved first, then a change of inversion, then a change of key colour, then a change in the number of notes. A silent arrival sends no MIDI, so the "arrive early" step is made checkable by the clock. Chord A sounds on beat 1 at crotchet = 60 and chord B must sound on beat 4; when about nine landings in ten are clean it must sound on beat 3, then on beat 2, and then A is halved in length. Whatever is hit is held for the rest of the bar and shown as "one key short" or "two keys long", and the piano replays the target. A distance ladder (from a home note to the third, fifth, octave, tenth, twelfth and two octaves, first in order, then shuffled with the target shown a beat ahead) gives the owner's own curve of error against distance and direction. The left-hand form, bass then chord, is modelled on Fitch's waltz practice and Kovarsky's beginner stride pattern ([Fitch 2012](https://practisingthepiano.com/leaps-of-faith-on-practising-waltzes/), [Kovarsky](https://hub.yamaha.com/keyboards/k-how-to/the-basics-of-stride-piano)): a low root on beat 1 and a close triad about an octave higher on beats 2 and 3, first on the tonic in C, G and F, then alternating tonic and dominant, starting at crotchet = 60, a half to a third of a real waltz's pace; two-note landings come before three-note ones, because two notes are easier to land on ([Piano With Jonny](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/learn-how-to-play-stride-piano/)).

Jumps add three scores to the chord-change set: the size and direction of a miss in keys, travel time from the release of A to the first press of B, and accuracy kept apart for outward and inward moves and for looking and not looking. The look condition is either declared or forced, by showing each target only shortly before it is due. Difficulty is ordered by required hand speed, distance divided by the time allowed. By this research's arithmetic, at an average 13.7 mm a semitone ([Parncutt et al. 1997](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/parncutt_richard/Pdfs/PaSlClRaDe97_FingeringModel.pdf)), triad inversions with half a second of rest need about 0.1 m/s, an octave in half a second 0.33 m/s, and the leap Bangert's students could not make about 1.1 m/s, which marks the corner to stay well away from. A black key is the narrower target, about 13.7 mm against 23.5 mm ([Wikipedia, Musical keyboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_keyboard)), and by Fitts' index of difficulty an octave onto a black key rates about the same as two octaves onto a white one. Fitts' law ([Wikipedia, Fitts's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitts%27s_law)) has not been tested on piano leaps in any study found, so the owner's logged curve should replace the index as soon as it exists.

### The other builds reuse judges Prima already has

Contrary motion, chromatic scales, staccato, a set dynamic and a crescendo are overlays on the Scales drill, scored by the evenness, legato, loudness and hand-sync measures it already computes; staccato adds key-down time as a share of the gap between notes, where measured natural staccato is about 40% and C. P. E. Bach's rule is under half ([Bresin 2000](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/publications/thesisrb/virtual%20virtuosity%20KTHB.pdf)). A further overlay answers the one thing the tempo research does show, that what is learned at one tempo carries only partly to another: a few notes at the target tempo landing on the beat, lengthened a note at a time, alongside the slow runs ([Pianote, Rudess](https://www.pianote.com/blog/how-to-play-scales-fast/)). Hands-differ variants score each hand's touch separately. Pedal changes need only the order of four events at each chord change. In the one study found, pedal timing followed no simple rule and differed strikingly between its two pianists ([Repp 1996](https://doi.org/10.1177/0305735696242011)), so feedback should name the event out of order, not count milliseconds; the MIDI standard treats the pedal as a switch at 64 ([MIDI Association](https://midi.org/midi-1-0-control-change-messages)). Voicing is a velocity difference between the top note and the rest.

### One adaptation scheme for all of them

These rules are proposals. Within a sitting, tempo follows accuracy in both directions: up one metronome mark after four clean runs in a row, down one mark after a failed run. By the logic of up-down rules ([Levitt 1971](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1912375)) that settles where four clean runs in a row is an even chance, at about 84% clean runs, which is Prima's 85% target with no extra machinery; it also matches Estrin's four clean repetitions before moving on. The 84% is this research's arithmetic, the 85% itself is borrowed from a theory of classification learning ([Wilson et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12552-4)), and no experiment compares rules for raising tempo. A clean run has no wrong, missed or extra note and every measure inside its band, which keeps most practice trials correct, as Duke's result asks. For landing drills the unit is the landing: about nine clean in the last ten moves the pause or the distance on.

Across days the rule is stricter. An item counts as held only when the day's first runs, after one unscored run-in, are clean at that tempo, and a rung of a ladder (a new distance, hands together, eyes on the screen) is climbed only after nine clean landings in ten on two different days. A new pattern is heard on the piano first, learned in Wait for me, and kept in a short block; only one new pattern enters per sitting. Once it is playable it rotates with others between runs, with a line telling the owner that mixed practice feels worse and works better. A set ends on stability, or on the signs of fatigue that MIDI can see, velocity sagging and notes dropping out ([Goubault et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8047012/)), and the technique step stays at three to five minutes by rotating one line item and one chord item a day instead of adding time.

## Seven builds in order, with the evidence stated plainly

No recommendation below rests on a trial showing that the exercise improves playing; none exists for any exercise. The labels say what each one does rest on. **Moderate** means one or a few controlled studies; **Weak** a single small study, a survey or a correlation; **Tradition** syllabuses, method books and teachers' opinion with no outcome data; **Proposal** this research's own design, untested. Nothing here reaches **Strong**.

Some things are left out on purpose. The leap literature leans on the octave (Fitch's thumb as a gauge, Bangert's task, Ortmann's test movement), but octaves and wide chords are where overuse cases cluster, so the first builds use single notes and close-position triads, and octave variants wait until the owner's hand takes an octave without stretching, to be played a few times, quietly. Double notes, tremolo and stretches sit above grade 5 in every source. Hanon-style sequences at a strong volume, and more held-finger figures, run against the force and fatigue evidence. Repeated notes with changing fingers teach a finger change that MIDI cannot see. The guitar world's one-minute chord-change count ([JustinGuitar](https://justinguitar.com/guitar-lessons/one-minute-changes-exercise-b1-110), seen in a search excerpt only) has no piano equivalent in any source found; Prima should keep its useful half, a log of the fastest clean tempo for each chord pair that shows which pairs are slowest, and drop the race, since sprinting for a count invites the errors and tension the evidence warns against.

Beyond the table, four limits apply to the whole report. The web-search allowance ran out part-way through every strand, so several sources were read only as abstracts or search excerpts; those are marked where used or left out. The ABRSM syllabus was read from a copy of ABRSM's own PDF hosted by a retailer, AMEB's lists were not obtained, and Trinity's composed exercises, which are in copyright, were not examined. The books of Sándor, Matthay, Neuhaus and Roskell could not be read directly, and no norms exist for learners at grades 1 to 5 on any measure, so every band is anchored on experts. And none of the proposed drills, thresholds or orders has been tested on anyone.

## Conclusion

The literature cannot say which exercise works, and that turns the job around: Prima can find out for its one learner. If every landing is logged with its distance, direction, key colour, look condition, tempo and day, a few weeks of chord drills will show whether jumps improve with the prepare-and-shrink routine, whether playing without looking lags and then closes, and which chord pairs stay slow: questions no published study has answered for a learner at this level. The logging and the cold first run of the day are therefore worth building before the catalogue grows, and the first task is ten minutes at the piano with the Keyboard check, because what the P-225 sends for releases, the pedal and notes struck together decides which of these judges are honest.

The owner's YouTube idea survives in a better form than scraping. What the law leaves free in a tutorial is exactly what a generator needs: a paragraph that names the pattern, the hands, the keys, the ladder and what to listen for. Once chord shapes and their moves join the cells Prima already transposes, each new exercise is one such paragraph, written after watching a lesson, and the app's advantage over both the exercise books and the commercial apps is the same in every case: it holds the practice to short, quiet, correct runs, and it measures what teachers only ask students to listen for.
