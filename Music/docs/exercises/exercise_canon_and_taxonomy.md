# Piano technique exercises: the canon, the exam syllabuses and a taxonomy (for Prima)

How these notes were made (read this first): the exam requirements below were read from the boards' own syllabus PDFs by extracting their text (ABRSM 2025 & 2026, Trinity "from 2023" online edition February 2026, RCM 2022 Edition). Publication years and composers' dates come from IMSLP work and composer pages; prefaces and contents lists come from public-domain scans and typeset sources (Schirmer Hanon, Schmitt, Joseffy, Kullak; Mutopia's Hanon). The session's web-search budget ran out part-way, so some items could only be read as search-result snippets (marked "snippet, page not opened") and a few could not be verified at all (listed under Gaps). abrsm.org and ameb.edu.au refused automated reading (Cloudflare), and piano.ameb.edu.au is outside the session's allowed network; the ABRSM syllabus was therefore read from a copy of ABRSM's PDF hosted by a UK music shop, and AMEB's grade-by-grade key lists were not obtained.

## 1. What are the standard categories of piano technique that exercises train? (taxonomy, with example patterns in words)

### Takeaway
The exercise literature and the exam boards carve technique into the same two dozen headings: five-finger work, thumb passage, scales (similar, contrary, chromatic, in double notes), arpeggios and broken chords, solid chords with inversions and cadences, repeated notes, trills, held-note independence, stretches, double notes, octaves and wrist work, leaps and hand-crossing, touch (legato/staccato), voicing, dynamics, rhythm between the hands, and pedalling. For grades 1 to 5 the boards examine only the first third of that list (scales, contrary motion, chromatic, arpeggios or broken chords, with staccato and a diminished seventh arriving at grade 4 to 5); Trinity and AMEB add short composed exercises for tone/voicing, coordination and wrist flexibility, and RCM adds solid and broken chords with inversions and cadences.

### Cited Findings

#### Where the headings come from (primary lists of categories)
- Joseffy's *School of Advanced Piano Playing* (1902) is organised by technique; its contents page lists: five-finger exercises; three-finger exercises with supporting finger; passing under and over; scale-exercises; trills; arpeggios; thirds; sixths; octaves; chromatic exercises; changing fingers on one key (repetitions); repetitions without changing fingers (light wrist-work); independence and strength of the fingers (paired notes); wrist-exercises; chords; alternation and interlacing of the hands; various styles of touch; thumb-exercises; sliding from the black keys; the glissando; skips; embellishments; extended chords and figures; stretches; exercises in "piano" and "pianissimo"; rhythmical studies — [Internet Archive scan, contents page](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)
- Plaidy's *Technische Studien* (1852) is in four parts: I. exercises without moving the hand, with the hand firmly fixed, with the hand moving, tremolo; II. scales; III. arpeggios; IV. connected thirds, fourths and sixths, scales in thirds, fourths, fifths, sixths and octaves, staccato double notes, chords and octaves — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Technische_Studien_f%C3%BCr_das_Pianofortespiel_%28Plaidy,_Louis%29)
- Liszt's *Technische Studien* (12 books in the Schuberth edition) run: exercises for strength and independence of each finger with quiet hand, and chord studies; preparatory studies for the major and minor scales; scales in thirds and sixths, arpeggios; chromatic scales, scales in contrary motion; repeated thirds, fourths and sixths; scales in double thirds and sixths; scales in chords of the sixth, chromatic thirds/fourths/sixths, octave scales; broken octaves, chord studies, shakes (trills) in thirds, sixths, fourths and octaves; diminished-seventh chords, exercises with quiet hand; broken chords through all keys; arpeggios in thirds and sixths; octave studies and chord studies — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Technische_Studien,_S.146_%28Liszt,_Franz%29)
- Beringer's *Daily Technical Studies* has twelve sections: I five-finger studies; II finger studies with progressive movement of the hand; III scale passages; IV chord passages; V changing fingers on one key; VI thirds, sixths and chord combinations; VII octave and chord studies; VIII extension studies; IX crossing and changing hands; X playing different rhythms with both hands together; XI a complete manual of scales and arpeggios; XII modulatory examples — [Alfred/Kalmus listing](https://www.alfred.com/products/beringer-daily-technical-studies-for-piano-00-pk-0002730)
- Isidor Philipp re-ordered Brahms's 51 Exercises into six technical sections: extension of the fingers while holding notes; scales; passage of the thumb and arpeggios; double notes; evenness of the fingers; trills — [IMSLP, note on the Philipp edition](https://imslp.org/wiki/51_Exercises,_WoO_6_%28Brahms,_Johannes%29)
- Trinity's composed exam exercises are grouped under three aims: "tone, balance and voicing", "coordination", and "finger & wrist strength and flexibility" — [Trinity Piano Syllabus from 2023, online edition Feb 2026](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)
- RCM's technical tests are grouped as scales (pentascales, two- and four-octave, formula pattern, chromatic, later in octaves and separated by a 3rd/6th), chords (triad sequence, tonic triads broken and solid/blocked with inversions, dominant 7th and leading-tone diminished 7th chords, four-note chords, chord progressions) and arpeggios — [RCM Piano Syllabus 2022 Edition](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- Hanon states his book's aims as "agility, independence, strength and perfect evenness in the fingers, as well as suppleness of the wrists", and says it makes the left hand as skilful as the right — [Schirmer edition, preface](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Faber's beginner technique books name physical habits rather than note patterns: Primer — good posture, rounded hand shape, firm fingertips, arm weight, correct thumb position; Level 1 — round hand shape, relaxed wrist, light hand bounce, finger independence; Level 2A — firm fingertips, light thumb, fast fingers, hands-together coordination, wrist float-off; Level 2B — arm weight, slur gesture, light thumb, connected pedaling — [Faber Technique Secrets: Primer](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/primer-level/), [Level 1](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/level-1/), [Level 2A](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/level-2a/), [Level 2B](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/level-2b/)

#### The taxonomy, one category at a time (what it trains; example patterns in words; level)

**A. Five-finger patterns (pentascales; hand stays in one position)**
- Hanon No. 1 (verified note by note from the typeset source): time 2/4, continuous sixteenth notes, both hands an octave apart. Ascending bar = scale degrees 1–3–4–5–6–5–4–3 (C E F G A G F E), right hand fingered 1-2-3-4-5-4-3-2, left hand 5-4-3-2-1-2-3-4. Each following bar repeats the eight-note cell one white key higher, for 14 bars (starting notes C up to the B an octave and a seventh above). Then the descending cell 5–3–2–1–7–1–2–3 from G (G E D C B C D E), right hand 5-4-3-2-1-2-3-4, left hand 1-2-3-4-5-4-3-2, stepping down one white key per bar for 15 bars; the exercise ends on a held C. Tempo "M.M. quarter = 60 to 108" — [Mutopia LilyPond source of Part 1](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/HanonCL/virtuoso-pianist-pt1/)
- Hanon No. 2 has the same construction with a different cell: ascending C E A G F G F E (right hand 1-2-5-4-3-4-3-2), descending G D B C D C D E (right hand 5-2-1-2-3-2-3-4) — [Mutopia source](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/HanonCL/virtuoso-pianist-pt1/)
- Hanon No. 5: ascending cell C A G A F G E F (right hand 1-5-4-5-3-4-2-3), descending cell C D C E D F E G (right hand 1-2-1-3-2-4-3-5); Hanon labels it "preparation for the trill with the 4th and 5th fingers of the right hand" — [Mutopia source](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/HanonCL/virtuoso-pianist-pt1/), [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- All of Hanon Nos. 1–20 are built this way: a sequence of eight sixteenth notes starting on C, repeated starting on D and so on across two octaves, then reversed back down — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_in_60_Exercises)
- Hanon's stated purpose per exercise, Part I: No. 1 stretch between 5th and 4th fingers; No. 2 fingers 3-4; No. 3 fingers 2-3-4; No. 4 "special exercise for the 3rd, 4th and 5th fingers"; No. 5 trill preparation for 4-5; No. 6 finger 5; No. 7 fingers 3-4-5 ("of the greatest importance"); No. 8 all five fingers; Nos. 10 and 11 and one later number are further "preparations for the trill" for 3-4 and 4-5; Nos. 9, 12, 17 and 20 and one number between them are headed "extension" (stretch) between named finger pairs (the scan's text layer drops some exercise numbers); he says the weak 4th and 5th fingers are the target of everything up to No. 31 — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Schmitt Op. 16: 213 short one-position exercises; instruction: repeat each at least ten or twenty times, hands separately then together, hands "steady and quiet", slowly at first, then faster; also practise them in other keys without changing the fingering; Nos. 119–127 also staccato — [Schirmer edition scan](https://archive.org/details/preparatoryexerc16schm)
- RCM "pentascales": tonic up to dominant and back, hands separately, ending with a solid (blocked) root-position triad; required legato and staccato; Preparatory A in C, G, D major and A minor, Preparatory B in D, A, F major and E, D minor — [RCM Syllabus 2022](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- Faber Adult Book 1 spends Units 1–10 in pentascale positions (C pentascale warm-up in Unit 1, treble C pentascale in Unit 8, G pentascales in three locations in Unit 9, "Major Pentascale Study" in Unit 10) before the first full scale in Unit 12 — [Faber Adult Book 1 page index](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1302/)
- Level: pre-grade-1 to grade 2 as exam material (RCM Preparatory A/B only); Hanon says his book "may be taken up after the pupil has studied about a year" — [RCM Syllabus 2022](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf), [Schirmer Hanon preface](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)

**B. Passing the thumb under (preparation for scales and arpeggios)**
- Hanon Nos. 32–37 are headed "turning the thumb under": under the 2nd finger, the 3rd, the 4th, the 5th ("of the highest importance"), "another example", and a special exercise in which three notes are held down silently in each hand while the thumb works; No. 38 prepares the scales — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Schmitt Op. 16 follows the five-finger exercises with "exercises for passing the thumb under the fingers, preparatory to the practice of the scales and arpeggios" — [Schirmer scan](https://archive.org/details/preparatoryexerc16schm), [IMSLP edition note](https://imslp.org/wiki/Etudes,_Op.16_%28Schmitt,_Aloys%29)
- Faber's Level 2B lesson books present one-octave scales in C, G and F major with "crossing the thumb under"; the matching technique habit is "light thumb" — [Faber Level 2B](https://pianoadventures.com/piano-books/basic-piano-adventures/level-2b/), [Technique Secrets 2B](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/level-2b/)

**C. Scales in similar motion**
- Definition used by the boards: ascend and descend the stated range in even notes, hands one octave apart when together, from memory, legato unless staccato is specified, without pedal; any fingering that works is accepted — [ABRSM Piano Syllabus 2025 & 2026](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf)
- Ladder across the grades: one octave hands separately (ABRSM Initial; RCM Preparatory B) → two octaves hands separately (ABRSM Grade 1; RCM Level 1) → two octaves hands together (ABRSM Grades 2–5, key by key; Trinity from Grade 2; RCM from Level 3) → four octaves (ABRSM and Trinity Grade 6; RCM Level 8) — [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf), [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079), [RCM](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- Hanon No. 39 writes out the 12 major and 12 minor scales, each major followed by its relative minor in both harmonic and melodic forms — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Staccato scales enter at ABRSM Grade 5 (A flat major and F minor, hands separately) and Trinity Grade 4 (legato or staccato) — [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf), [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)

**D. Scales in contrary motion, and the "formula pattern"**
- ABRSM: both hands start on the same tonic (unison) and move outwards and back; five notes at Initial Grade, one octave at Grade 1 (C major), two octaves from Grade 2; a harmonic minor in contrary motion appears at Grade 4 (C harmonic minor) — [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf)
- RCM formula pattern (Level 2 upward): hands start in parallel; at the halfway point of the ascent they go out and back in contrary motion; the ascent is finished in parallel; coming down, halfway there is another contrary-motion out-and-back, then the final descent in parallel; two octaves at Levels 2–7, four octaves at Level 8 — [Piano Pantry guide](https://pianopantry.com/visual-guide-formula-pattern-scales/), [RCM Syllabus 2022](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- Faber Adult Book 1 Unit 5's technique page pairs a study called "Parallel Promenade" with one called "Contrary Cakewalk" — [Faber Book 1, p. 68](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1302/p68/)

**E. Chromatic scales**
- ABRSM order: Grade 2 chromatic from D, one octave, hands separately; Grade 3 chromatic in contrary motion from D (unison start); Grade 4 chromatic in similar motion from F sharp, two octaves, hands together; Grade 5 chromatic in contrary motion with the hands starting a major third apart (left hand F sharp, right hand A sharp) — [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf)
- Trinity starts earlier: Grade 1 chromatic in contrary motion from D, hands together; Grade 2 similar motion from B flat — [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)
- Hanon No. 40 gives chromatic scales, including "in contrary motion, beginning on the octave" — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)

**F. Scales in double notes and scales a third or sixth apart**
- "A third apart" (two hands, one note each, a third between them, tonic as the lower note) and "a sixth apart" (tonic as the upper note) are Grade 7 and Grade 8 items at ABRSM; "scale in thirds" (one hand playing both notes) is required legato and staccato at Grade 7 in G major, hands separately — [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf)
- Trinity asks for C major in thirds, one octave, hands separately at Grade 6 — [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)
- Hanon: No. 48 detached thirds and sixths from the wrist; No. 50 legato thirds (hold the upper note for an instant while the other fingers pass to the next third); No. 52 scales in thirds "in the keys most used" — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Level: advanced, a ladder marker only; double-note scales are first examined at Trinity Grade 6 and ABRSM Grade 7, and sit in Hanon's Part III — [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079), [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf), [Schirmer Hanon](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)

**G. Arpeggios and broken chords**
- ABRSM: Initial Grade "arpeggio" to a fifth (the printed pattern has five notes: up tonic–third–fifth and back), Grade 1 one octave hands separately, Grade 2 two octaves hands separately, Grades 3–5 two octaves hands together key by key; diminished seventh at Grade 5; dominant sevenths resolving on the tonic at Grade 6; inversions at Grades 7–8 — [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf)
- Trinity: Initial "broken triad" to the fifth; Grade 1 broken chords in triplets over one octave (the only non-duple rhythm in Trinity's scale work); arpeggios from Grade 2 — [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)
- RCM: tonic triads "broken" in triplets, root position then inversions, one octave (Preparatory B to Level 2) then two octaves; tonic arpeggios in root position from Level 4 — [RCM Syllabus](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf), [ColorInMyPiano charts](https://colorinmypiano.com/wp-content/files/RCM_2022_Technical_Requirements_Charts_Prep-10.pdf)
- Hanon: No. 41 arpeggios on the triads in 24 keys; No. 42 diminished-seventh arpeggios and No. 43 dominant-seventh arpeggios, both described as "extension (stretching) of the fingers" — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Faber Adult Book 2: Unit 4 technique page has major triads and minor triads each followed by a "broken chord variation" and a "L.H. octave variation"; Unit 6 "Alberti Bass Study"; Unit 8 "Broken-Chord Etude" and "Rippling Triplets"; Unit 15 one-octave arpeggios with "Arpeggio Power" for each hand — [Faber Book 2 index](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1334/), [p. 50](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1334/p50/)
- ABRSM dropped broken chords entirely when its scale requirements were revised for 2021 — [Pianodao review](https://pianodao.com/2020/07/12/abrsm-piano-scales-2021/)

**H. Solid (blocked) chords, inversions and cadences**
- RCM Preparatory A "triad sequence": root-position triads on each white key in turn, one octave ascending in C major, hands separately, first broken (triplets) then solid — [RCM Syllabus](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf), [charts](https://colorinmypiano.com/wp-content/files/RCM_2022_Technical_Requirements_Charts_Prep-10.pdf)
- RCM Levels 1–6: tonic triads solid/blocked, root position and inversions (with rests between chords at Levels 1–4, without rests at Levels 5–6); from Level 5 the pattern ends with a I–V–I progression (i–V–i in minor); Level 7 I–IV–V–I; Level 8 adds a cadential 6/4 — [RCM Syllabus, technical tests and examples pages](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- RCM "tonic four-note chords" start at Level 7, broken, with inversions, two octaves hands together; dominant 7th chords broken and solid start at Level 5; leading-tone diminished 7th chords at Level 6 — [RCM Syllabus](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- Faber Adult: "C chord warm-up" (Book 1 Unit 4), "the G7 chord" (Unit 13), "primary chords in C major" with a "IV chord warm-up" (Unit 14), primary chords in G (Unit 16); Book 2 teaches I–IV and I–IV–V7 "in close position" in F major, A minor, D minor, E minor and D major, and triad inversions in Unit 5 — [Book 1 index](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1302/), [Book 2 index](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1334/)

**I. Repeated notes**
- Hanon No. 44 repeats each note in groups of three, No. 45 in groups of two "by all five fingers" with six alternative fingerings, No. 47 in groups of four; instruction: lift the fingers high without raising hand or wrist — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Beringer gives a whole section to "changing fingers on one key"; Joseffy separates "changing fingers on one key (repetitions)" from "repetitions without changing fingers (light wrist-work)" — [Alfred listing](https://www.alfred.com/products/beringer-daily-technical-studies-for-piano-00-pk-0002730), [Joseffy contents](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)

**J. Trills and ornaments**
- Hanon No. 46 is "the trill for all five fingers" (he notes Mozart used the exercise); Part I already contains "preparations for the trill" for fingers 3-4 and 4-5; Part III adds "the fourfold trill in thirds, for all five fingers" with special fingerings — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- The book as a whole is summarised as covering thumb crossing, strengthening of the fourth and fifth fingers, and quadruple and triple trills — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_in_60_Exercises)
- Joseffy has separate chapters for "trills" and "embellishments" — [Joseffy contents](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)
- A Kalmus edition of Czerny's Op. 599 lists its contents as running through easy tunes, rests and "exercises of velocity" to melodies with and without ornaments — [retailer listing of the Kalmus edition, snippet, page not opened](https://performersmusicchicago.com/practical-method-for-beginners-op-599-alf-00-k03346)

**K. Held-note finger independence**
- Dohnányi's first exercise has some fingers pressed and held while the weakest fingers trill; the book's first part is "finger independence and strengthening" and includes holding some notes while playing others — [review article](https://classicalmusicpaolo.substack.com/p/essential-finger-exercises-by-ernst)
- Liszt's Book 1 is "exercises for gaining strength and independence of each individual finger with quiet hand" — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Technische_Studien,_S.146_%28Liszt,_Franz%29)
- Plaidy's Part I includes "exercises with the hand firmly fixed" — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Technische_Studien_f%C3%BCr_das_Pianofortespiel_%28Plaidy,_Louis%29)
- Joseffy: "three-finger exercises with supporting finger"; annotations "in this first exercise hold each finger down firmly" and (in the German text) "Die kleinen Noten werden nicht angeschlagen", the small notes are not struck — [Joseffy scan](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)
- Hanon's thumb exercise has three notes held down silently in each hand for twelve bars — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)

**L. Stretches (extensions)**
- Hanon No. 49: "stretches from the 1st to the 4th fingers, and from the 2nd to the 5th, in each hand" — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Beringer Section VIII "extension studies"; Joseffy "stretches" and "extended chords and figures" — [Alfred listing](https://www.alfred.com/products/beringer-daily-technical-studies-for-piano-00-pk-0002730), [Joseffy contents](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)

**M. Octaves and wrist work**
- Hanon No. 51 preparatory exercise for scales in octaves (supple wrists, firm fingers, unoccupied fingers slightly rounded; black keys taken with the 4th finger), No. 53 scales in octaves in 24 keys, No. 56 scales in broken octaves, No. 57 broken arpeggios in octaves, No. 58 held octaves with detached notes inside them, No. 60 tremolo — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Kullak's *School of Octave Playing* has three parts: preparatory exercises for developing the hand for octaves, seven octave studies, and about 60 passages from the repertoire — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Die_Schule_des_Octavenspiels,_Op.48_%28Kullak,_Theodor%29)
- Kullak's first drills: repeated striking of one key from the wrist; then a fragment of the chromatic scale in octaves, fingers 1-5 on white keys and 1-4 on black, the hand low on white keys and high on black — [Schirmer scan](https://archive.org/details/schoolofoctavepl00kull)
- RCM first requires scales in octaves at Level 9 (blocked staccato, or broken legato for small hands) — [ColorInMyPiano charts](https://colorinmypiano.com/wp-content/files/RCM_2022_Technical_Requirements_Charts_Prep-10.pdf)
- Level: advanced, beyond grade 5; no board examines octave scales before RCM Level 9, and ABRSM and Trinity do not ask for them at any grade — [ColorInMyPiano charts](https://colorinmypiano.com/wp-content/files/RCM_2022_Technical_Requirements_Charts_Prep-10.pdf), [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf), [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)

**N. Leaps, skips, hand-crossing and alternating hands**
- Joseffy has chapters on "skips" (with the note "in skips, use the 5th finger only") and "alternation and interlacing of the hands"; Beringer Section IX is "crossing and changing hands" — [Joseffy scan](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose), [Alfred listing](https://www.alfred.com/products/beringer-daily-technical-studies-for-piano-00-pk-0002730)
- Faber Adult Book 1 Unit 7's technique page carries an audio track named "Cross-Hand Arpeggios"; Book 2 Unit 5's is "1st Inversion Toss" — [Book 1 p. 88](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1302/p88/), [Book 2 p. 60](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1334/p60/)

**O. Touch: legato, staccato, slurs, wrist release**
- ABRSM: everything legato to Grade 4; staccato scales from Grade 5; legato or staccato at the examiner's choice from Grade 6 — [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf)
- Trinity: legato to Grade 3; legato or staccato scales from Grade 4 and arpeggios from Grade 5 — [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)
- RCM asks for staccato as well as legato pentascales at the Preparatory levels — [RCM Syllabus](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- Faber names "wrist float-off" (Level 2A), "light hand bounce" and "relaxed wrist" (Level 1) and "slur gesture" (Level 2B) as the habits behind these touches — [Level 1](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/level-1/), [2A](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/level-2a/), [2B](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/level-2b/)
- Joseffy has a chapter "various styles of touch" — [Joseffy contents](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)

**P. Voicing and balance**
- One of Trinity's three exercise groups at every grade is "tone, balance and voicing" — [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)
- Joseffy's exercises carry instructions such as "the inner parts very distinct" and "all tones of equal force. Through all keys" — [Joseffy scan](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)

**Q. Dynamic control**
- Trinity attaches dynamics to scales: mf at Initial and Grade 1; f or p from Grade 2; at Grades 7–8 also a crescendo and diminuendo (p–f–p) across the scale — [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)
- ABRSM's revised requirements have no scales with varied dynamics at any grade — [Pianodao review](https://pianodao.com/2020/07/12/abrsm-piano-scales-2021/)
- Joseffy: "exercises in piano and pianissimo"; "also without accent, forte and piano" — [Joseffy scan](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)
- Faber Adult Book 2 opens with a "Dynamic Study" — [Book 2 index](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1334/)

**R. Hand independence, coordination and rhythm against rhythm**
- Trinity's second exercise group at every grade is "coordination" — [Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=9079)
- Beringer Section X is "playing different rhythms with both hands together" — [Alfred listing](https://www.alfred.com/products/beringer-daily-technical-studies-for-piano-00-pk-0002730)
- Faber: "hands-together coordination" is a Level 2A habit; Adult Book 1 has an "Imitative Study" (Unit 8) and rhythm drills (Units 6–7); Book 2 ends with a "Rhythm Trainer" — [Technique Secrets 2A](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/level-2a/), [Book 1 index](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1302/), [Book 2 p. 184](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1334/p184/)
- Joseffy closes with "rhythmical studies" — [Joseffy contents](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)

**S. Pedalling**
- Exam scales are played without pedal — [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf)
- Faber Adult Book 2 teaches it as technique: "Pedal Exercise", then "Pedaling the Scale" and "Pedaling Intervals" on the Unit 2 technique page, "Pedaling Chords", and a "Pedal Study" in Unit 11; "connected pedaling" is a Level 2B technique secret — [Book 2 index](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1334/), [p. 26](https://pianoadventures.com/qr/ff1334/p26/), [Technique Secrets 2B](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/level-2b/)

**T. Whole-arm habits (posture, arm weight, relaxed wrist)**
- Faber puts these first: Primer level's five secrets are posture, rounded hand, firm fingertips, arm weight and thumb position — [Technique Secrets Primer](https://pianoadventures.com/technique-secrets/primer-level/)
- Hanon's instructions are the opposite school: "lift the fingers high and with precision", hands and wrists still except in the wrist exercises — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Critics of finger-drill books: Abby Whiteside rejected finger independence as a goal; Hao Huang finds Hanon, Schmitt and Czerny useful for beginners but warns against "hours spent mindlessly going over finger patterns"; Dorothy Taubman opposed technical exercises altogether — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_in_60_Exercises)
- György Sándor's *On Piano Playing* stresses gravity as the source of energy and criticises excessive muscular tension — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gy%C3%B6rgy_S%C3%A1ndor)

### Inferences
- A generator needs only four ingredients to cover almost every category above: (1) a short cell of scale degrees with a fingering, (2) a transposition rule (step up the scale each bar like Hanon; or move the whole cell through the keys like Pischna and Schmitt's "practise in other keys with the same fingering"), (3) a mirror rule for the left hand (same notes an octave lower with mirrored fingers, as in Hanon), and (4) a touch/dynamic/rhythm overlay (legato, staccato, f/p, crescendo, dotted or triplet groupings). This follows from how Hanon, Schmitt and the boards specify their material.
- For an ABRSM grade 1–5 learner who already has scale, arpeggio, five-finger and trill drills, the untouched categories that the sources place at this level are: contrary-motion and chromatic patterns (ABRSM Grades 1–5), staccato scales (Grade 5 / Trinity Grade 4), solid and broken triads with inversions and I–V–I / I–IV–V7 cadences (RCM Levels 1–6, Faber Adult Books 1–2), thumb-under preparations (Hanon 32–38), repeated notes with changing fingers (Hanon 44–47), hand-coordination studies (Trinity group 2, Faber parallel/contrary and imitative studies), voicing studies (Trinity group 1), dynamics on scales (Trinity) and pedalling drills (Faber Book 2).
- Octaves, double-note scales, tremolo, glissando and wide stretches sit above grade 5 in every source consulted (ABRSM Grades 7–8, Trinity Grades 6–8, RCM Levels 9–10, Hanon Part III), so they are ladder markers rather than material for this learner.

### Gaps
- No single modern pedagogy textbook's classification was verified. Sándor's five "basic motions" (which I recall as free fall, five-fingers/scales/arpeggios, rotation, staccato and thrust) could not be confirmed: the Wikipedia article does not list them and the search budget ran out. RCM's syllabus lists Sándor's book in its bibliography, which is all that was verified.
- Cortot's five chapter headings could not be fully verified (see section 2).
- Forearm rotation, as a named category with a concrete exercise, did not turn up in any source read; it is implicit in broken-chord, Alberti-bass and tremolo material only by my own reading.
- No source was found that maps categories to minutes of daily practice.

## 2. What do the canonical collections contain, what is each meant to train, and at what level is it used?

### Takeaway
The collections fall into three kinds: pure pattern books that transpose a small cell (Hanon, Schmitt, Pischna, Beringer, Plaidy, Joseffy, Philipp, Dohnányi, Cortot, Liszt, Brahms), graded études that wrap one figure in a short piece (Czerny's ladder 599 → 849 → 299 → 740, Duvernoy, Burgmüller, Cramer, Clementi), and a child-facing daily warm-up series (Burnam). Only Hanon Part I and II, Schmitt, Czerny Op. 599/821/849, Duvernoy, Burgmüller Op. 100, the Little Pischna and Burnam sit in the late-beginner-to-intermediate band; the rest are advanced.

### Cited Findings

#### Hanon, *The Virtuoso Pianist in 60 Exercises*
- Three parts: Part I Nos. 1–20 "preparatory exercises"; Part II Nos. 21–43 "transcendent exercises for preparing the fingers for the virtuoso exercises"; Part III Nos. 44–60 "virtuoso exercises" — [Schirmer edition](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_in_60_Exercises)
- Part I: twenty five-finger sequences in C major built as described in section 1A; metronome 60 rising to 108; played in sets without stopping (1–2, then 3–5, and so on) — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Part II in order: Nos. 21 onward continue the sequences, now in bars of at least three beats, for fingers 3-4-5; trill preparations ("for all five fingers", "trill alternating between 1-2 and 4-5"); turning the thumb under (see 1B); the 12 major and 12 minor scales; chromatic scales; arpeggios on the triads in 24 keys; diminished-seventh and dominant-seventh arpeggios — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Part III in order: repeated notes in threes; repeated notes in twos with changing fingers; the trill for all five fingers; repeated notes in fours; detached thirds and sixths (wrist); stretches 1–4 and 2–5; legato thirds; preparatory exercise for octave scales; scales in thirds; scales in octaves in 24 keys; fourfold trill in thirds; scales in broken octaves; broken arpeggios in octaves; sustained octaves with detached inner notes; a trill study for the finger pairs 1-4 and 2-5; tremolo. Two headings in this run did not survive the scan's text layer; the usual contents list has a threefold trill after the fourfold trill in thirds and calls the 1-4/2-5 study the fourfold trill in sixths — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Daily use as Hanon intended: the whole book "can be played through in an hour"; once mastered it should be played through daily for a time; exercises already learned should be played at least once daily — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Level: "may be taken up after the pupil has studied about a year"; Part III is "considerably more difficult" and meant for after Parts I and II — [Schirmer preface](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_in_60_Exercises)
- RCM lists Hanon (Willan's edition and Berlin's *New Hanon*) among its recommended technique resources — [RCM Syllabus 2022, resources](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)

#### Aloys Schmitt, Op. 16 (Preparatory Exercises)
- Op. 16 is a set of études in three books; Book 1 opens with 213 "exercices préparatifs" (five-finger exercises) followed by 20 études; the Schirmer volume titled *Preparatory Exercises* prints the 213 numbered exercises with an appendix by A. Knecht — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Etudes,_Op.16_%28Schmitt,_Aloys%29)
- Aim, from the heading: "for acquiring the greatest possible independence and evenness of the fingers" — [Schirmer scan](https://archive.org/details/preparatoryexerc16schm)
- Practice instructions: see 1A (10–20 repetitions, hands separately then together, quiet hand, slow then faster, transpose with the same fingering); from No. 170 the exercises move "ascending and descending, the whole extent of the keyboard" — [Schirmer scan](https://archive.org/details/preparatoryexerc16schm)
- The Schirmer appendix adds thumb-under exercises, scales (with a note on the keys in which the thumbs of both hands fall together), scales in thirds and sixths, arpeggios "to be practised also in all keys with the C major fingering", dominant-seventh chord passages and chromatic scales in double thirds and sixths — [Schirmer scan](https://archive.org/details/preparatoryexerc16schm)

#### Czerny (five opus numbers)
- Op. 599, *Erster Wiener Lehrmeister* / *Practical Method for Beginners*: 100 exercises — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Erster_Wiener_Lehrmeister_im_Pianoforte-Spiel,_Op.599_%28Czerny,_Carl%29)
- Op. 599 is taught in blocks: Nos. 1–10, 11–18, 19–38, 39–57, 59–80, 80–100 (the division used by the Practising the Piano Online Academy's course on the book) — [Online Academy](https://dfp.informance.biz/content/pages/173-etudes-studies-czerny-part-1/866-book-structure-uses-1)
- Op. 599 starts with lessons for learning the notes and goes on through "exercises of velocity" to melodies with and without ornaments — [retailer listing of the Kalmus edition, snippet, page not opened](https://performersmusicchicago.com/practical-method-for-beginners-op-599-alf-00-k03346)
- A publisher's blurb describes Op. 599 as starting at a very simple level and progressing rapidly to intermediate level, for beginners who can already read music — [retailer listing, snippet, page not opened; which of the listings carried this wording is uncertain](https://www.musicroom.com/practical-method-for-beginners-op-599-hl50253050)
- Op. 821: 160 eight-bar exercises "for quickly learning the most necessary figures and passages, and for promoting sight-reading"; uses all major and minor keys — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/160_Kurze_%C3%9Cbungen,_Op.821_%28Czerny,_Carl%29)
- Op. 849, *30 Études de mécanisme*: 30 études, written "expressly for young pupils and to serve as an introduction to the studies of velocity"; the cover says "to serve as introduction to the School of Velocity, Op. 299"; keys stay within C, F, G, B flat, D, A, E, E flat, A flat major and G minor — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/30_%C3%89tudes_de_m%C3%A9canisme,_Op.849_%28Czerny,_Carl%29)
- Op. 299, *School of Velocity*: 40 pieces in four books, almost all marked Presto or Molto allegro; subtitle "exercises calculated to develop the evenness of the fingers" — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Die_Schule_der_Gel%C3%A4ufigkeit,_Op.299_%28Czerny,_Carl%29)
- Op. 740, *The Art of Finger Dexterity*: 50 studies in six books, titled as the "progressive continuation of the School of Velocity ... for the higher training of the pianist" — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/The_Art_of_Finger_Dexterity,_Op.740_%28Czerny,_Carl%29)
- Where an exam board places Czerny: RCM's 2022 étude lists use an Op. 139 étude at Level 2, Op. 718 No. 7 at Level 5, Op. 299 No. 3 at Level 8 and Op. 636 No. 6 at Level 9 — [RCM Syllabus 2022](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- Czerny wrote exercises "designed to cover from the first lessons for children up to the needs of the most advanced virtuoso", and his study books "are still widely used in piano teaching" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Czerny)

#### Burgmüller, Op. 100 (*25 Études faciles et progressives*)
- 25 titled character études "composed and fingered expressly for the reach of small hands": La candeur, L'arabesque, La pastorale, La petite réunion, Innocence, Progrès, Le courant limpide, La gracieuse, La chasse, Tendre fleur, La bergeronnette, L'adieu, Consolation, La styrienne, Ballade, Douce plainte, La babillarde, Inquiétude, Ave Maria, La tarentelle, L'harmonie des anges, Barcarolle, Le retour, L'hirondelle, La chevaleresque — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/25_%C3%89tudes_faciles_et_progressives,_Op.100_%28Burgm%C3%BCller,_Friedrich%29)
- Level: progressive from about ABRSM Grade 2 at the start to about Grade 5 by the end; suggested use is to keep three or four in rotation as daily practice — [Practising the Piano Online Academy](https://www.informance.biz/online-academy/page/132-introduction)
- Current exam placements: ABRSM 2025 & 2026 lists Ballade (No. 15) at Grade 4 and La tarentelle (No. 20) at Grade 5; RCM 2022 uses Arabesque (No. 2) at Level 3, Ballade at Level 4 and Harmony of the Angels (No. 21) at Level 5 — [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf), [RCM](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- The harder sequel, Op. 109, appears at ABRSM Grade 6 and RCM Levels 7–8 — [ABRSM](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf), [RCM](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)

#### Duvernoy, Op. 176 (*École primaire*)
- 25 studies; the German title calls it elementary instruction for first beginners "in 25 easy and progressive studies" — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_primaire,_Op.176_%28Duvernoy,_Jean-Baptiste%29)

#### Beringer, *Daily Technical Studies*
- Twelve sections (listed in section 1), described by the current publisher as running "from simple warm-ups through more advanced studies" — [Alfred/Kalmus](https://www.alfred.com/products/beringer-daily-technical-studies-for-piano-00-pk-0002730)

#### Pischna, *60 Progressive Exercises* (Tägliche Studien), and *The Little Pischna*
- 60 exercises; the usual edition is edited by Bernhard Wolff, and Hugo Riemann wrote the preparatory exercise for No. 60 and the scales in thirds — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/T%C3%A4gliche_Studien_%28Pischna,_Josef%29)
- "The exercises are transposed and written out in every key"; they progress steadily in difficulty and aim at hand strength, flexibility and finger dexterity — [Alfred edition description](https://www.popplersmusic.com/item/00-637/)
- Alfred's subtitle lists the content as studies on trills, scales, chords, passages and arpeggios, and the edition is rated late intermediate to advanced — [one of several retailer listings of the Alfred edition, snippet, page not opened](https://juilliardstore.com/products/pischna-technical-studies-00-637)
- *The Little Pischna*: 48 exercises compiled by Bernhard Wolff as an introduction to the 60 — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/The_Little_Pischna_%28Wolff,_Bernhard%29), [Schott listing, snippet, page not opened](https://www.schott-music.com/en/the-little-pischna-no185060.html)

#### Brahms, 51 Exercises (WoO 6)
- 51 numbered exercises in two books, many with variants (1a–f, 2a–b …); several are marked to be taken through "all keys" (Nos. 20, 32, 33, 35, 37, 49, 50); composed roughly 1850–93 — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/51_Exercises,_WoO_6_%28Brahms,_Johannes%29)
- They are not in progressive order, which is why Philipp regrouped them by technique (list in section 1) "while emphasizing rhythmic variety in each" — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/51_Exercises,_WoO_6_%28Brahms,_Johannes%29)

#### Liszt, *Technische Studien* (S.146)
- 68 studies in three books (12 books in the first, Schuberth, edition), composed 1868–73; contents by book are listed in section 1 — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Technische_Studien,_S.146_%28Liszt,_Franz%29)

#### Cortot, *Rational Principles of Pianoforte Technique*
- Five chapters with exercises — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Rational_Principles_of_Piano_Technique_%28Cortot,_Alfred%29)
- The publisher's blurb names the chapter subjects as including evenness, independence and mobility of the fingers; passing under of the thumb (scales and arpeggios); and the technique of double notes and polyphonic playing — [Musicroom listing, snippet cut short, page not opened](https://www.musicroom.com/rational-principles-of-pianoforte-technique-slb%2000413200)

#### Dohnányi, *Essential Finger Exercises*
- Forty exercises in three parts: finger independence and strengthening (held notes, diminished-chord shapes, finger crossings, repeated and broken-chord tremolos); scales and chords (contrary-motion scales and arpeggios, scales with crossed hands, broken chords and Alberti bass); double notes (turns in thirds, chromatic fourths, trills in sixths) — [review article](https://classicalmusicpaolo.substack.com/p/essential-finger-exercises-by-ernst)
- Written for advanced pianists maintaining their technique, not beginners; the preface asks for practice "through the fingers with the brain" and treats most routine exercise books as redundant — [review article](https://classicalmusicpaolo.substack.com/p/essential-finger-exercises-by-ernst)
- RCM lists it (as *Essential Finger Exercises for Obtaining a Sure Piano Technique*, Budapest 1929) among its technique resources — [RCM Syllabus 2022](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)

#### Philipp, *Exercises for Independence of the Fingers*
- 18 series of exercises, dedicated to Paderewski, published in English by G. Schirmer — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Exercises_for_Independence_of_the_Fingers_%28Philipp,_Isidor%29)

#### Joseffy, *School of Advanced Piano Playing* and *First Studies*
- The *School* (1902) is a chapter-per-technique exercise book (chapter list in section 1) with German and English instructions, many exercises marked "through all the keys", "each hand alone" or "left hand two octaves lower" — [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/schoolofadvanced00jose)
- *First Studies for the Piano: Advancing to a High Degree of Development* followed in 1913 — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/First_Studies_for_the_Piano_%28Joseffy,_Rafael%29)

#### Plaidy, *Technische Studien*
- Four parts (list in section 1); long kept in print by Schirmer in an English edition "augmented by Karl Klauser" — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Technische_Studien_f%C3%BCr_das_Pianofortespiel_%28Plaidy,_Louis%29)

#### Kullak, *School of Octave Playing*, Op. 48
- Three parts (preparatory exercises; seven octave études; about 60 repertoire passages); the text describes the wrist-stroke, distinguishes a "hammer-touch" from a "pressure-touch", treats "legato playing by the aid of the wrist", and names the thumb, then the fifth and fourth fingers, as the octave fingers — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Die_Schule_des_Octavenspiels,_Op.48_%28Kullak,_Theodor%29), [Schirmer scan](https://archive.org/details/schoolofoctavepl00kull)

#### Cramer, 84 Études (*Studio per il pianoforte*)
- 84 études in two sets of 42 (Op. 30 and Op. 40; some editions call the whole Op. 50) — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Studio_per_il_pianoforte_%28Cramer,_Johann_Baptist%29)
- "Still considered standard didactic works for piano students" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Baptist_Cramer)
- RCM places a Cramer Op. 30 étude at Level 9 — [RCM Syllabus 2022](https://rcmusic-kentico-cdn.s3.amazonaws.com/rcm/media/main/about%20us/rcm%20publishing/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- Dohnányi exempted Clementi, Cramer and Bertini from his view that study books were redundant — [review article](https://classicalmusicpaolo.substack.com/p/essential-finger-exercises-by-ernst)

#### Clementi, *Gradus ad Parnassum*, Op. 44
- 100 exercises "in the strict and in the elegant style", issued in three books — [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Gradus_ad_Parnassum,_Op.44_%28Clementi,_Muzio%29)
- Debussy's "Doctor Gradus ad Parnassum" parodies an exercise set of this name; Wikipedia's source attributes the target to Czerny's set, not Clementi's — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradus_ad_Parnassum)

#### Burnam, *A Dozen a Day*
- A series of six volumes including a Mini Book and a Preparatory Book, aimed at young children and beginners; the preface's aim is strong hands and flexible fingers; most exercises are in C major with little melodic content; each has a title and a stick-figure drawing by Burnam suggesting the touch; later parts cover scales, chromatic scales, arpeggios and ornaments — [PTNA Piano Encyclopedia](https://enc.piano.or.jp/en/persons/6835)
- Structure: groups of twelve exercises (Group I, Group II …); the publisher says to learn them two or three at a time, master one group before starting the next, and transpose them to other keys for extra challenge — [Hal Leonard / Willis](https://www.halleonard.com/product/404073)
- Original subtitle: "technical exercises for the piano to be done each day before practicing" — [Internet Archive catalogue record](https://archive.org/details/dozendaytechnica0000burn)

### Inferences
- Level bands that follow from the cited placements: late beginner (about ABRSM 1–2): Schmitt's five-finger exercises, Hanon Part I, Czerny Op. 599, Duvernoy Op. 176, Burnam, Burgmüller Nos. 1–8 or so. Early intermediate (about ABRSM 3–5): Hanon Part II (thumb, scales, arpeggios), Czerny Op. 821 and Op. 849, the rest of Burgmüller Op. 100, the Little Pischna. Beyond grade 5: Czerny Op. 299 (RCM Level 8) and Op. 740, Pischna's 60, Beringer's later sections, Cramer (RCM Level 9), Clementi, Kullak, Philipp, Joseffy, Brahms, Liszt, Cortot, Dohnányi, Hanon Part III. The first two bands rest on the books' own titles and on ABRSM/RCM placements; they are my synthesis, not a published table.
- The pattern books share one transposition device the étude books lack: Pischna prints each exercise in every key, Brahms and Joseffy mark exercises "through all keys", Schmitt and Burnam tell the player to transpose with the same fingering. A generated exercise that walks a cell through the keys is squarely in this tradition.
- Czerny's own titles give the intended order of his ladder (Op. 849 "introduction to" Op. 299; Op. 740 "continuation of" Op. 299), so no outside authority is needed for that sequence.

### Gaps
- Burgmüller Op. 100: no source was read that states the technical point of each étude. My unverified recollection of the scores: No. 1 even legato eighths with crescendo; No. 2 fast five-finger sixteenth groups against staccato chords; No. 5 descending scale figures; No. 6 parallel scales in tenths and staccato; No. 9 broken octaves/sixths and staccato; No. 14 grace notes and waltz bass; No. 20 tarantella triplets; No. 21 arpeggios shared between the hands; No. 24 hand-crossing. Check against the scores before use.
- Czerny Op. 599: the internal section headings (five-note compass, quiet hand, bass clef, velocity, ornaments) were only seen in retailer snippets; the Online Academy page gives the block divisions but its content is behind a paywall.
- Cortot: chapters 4 and 5 (which I recall as extensions, and wrist technique with chords) and the book's daily-gymnastics preliminary were not verified; three retailer pages refused access.
- Philipp: what the 18 series consist of (I recall held-note patterns on diminished-seventh chord shapes) was not verified.
- Pischna: the note-level design of the early exercises (I recall held notes with moving fingers, each exercise modulating upward by semitone) was not verified.

## 3. What technical work do the exam boards require grade by grade (grades 1 to 5 especially)?

### Takeaway
ABRSM (2025 & 2026 syllabus, scale requirements unchanged since 2021 and unchanged again for 2027 & 2028) asks only for scales, contrary-motion and chromatic scales and arpeggios, from under ten items at Grade 1 to about thirty at Grade 5 by my count, with no broken chords and no exercises. Trinity (syllabus from 2023) asks for fewer keys but adds dynamics, staccato from Grade 4 and three composed exercises a grade. RCM (2022 Edition) is the fullest: pentascales, formula-pattern scales, and broken and solid triads with inversions and cadences at every level. AMEB (syllabus in force since 2019, technical work books dated 2018) requires scales, arpeggios and three set technical exercises per grade, but its key lists are printed only in the technical work books and were not obtained.

### Inferences
- For keys, ABRSM's Initial-to-Grade-5 sequence gives a ready-made order of difficulty for a grade 1–5 learner: C, G, F, D minor, A minor → D, A, E minor, G minor → B flat, E flat, B minor, C minor, E (contrary) → B, F sharp, A flat, F sharp minor, F minor → all the rest by Grade 5.
- Hands-together playing lags one grade behind hands-separate in ABRSM and RCM for each key group; an exercise generator can copy that staging (introduce a key hands separately, promote it to hands together later).
- The boards disagree on what belongs at grade 1: ABRSM has no chromatic or triad work there, Trinity has a contrary-motion chromatic and triplet broken chords, RCM has solid and broken triads with inversions. Material from any of the three is defensible for a late beginner.
- Trinity's and AMEB's decision to add three short composed exercises per grade (voicing, coordination, wrist) is the closest official precedent for Prima's plan to write its own exercises beyond scales.

## 4. Publication years, authors' death years, and public-domain status

### Takeaway
Everything on the list except Burnam's *A Dozen a Day* is in the public domain in the United States, where works published in 1930 or earlier are free as of 1 January 2026. In life-plus-70 countries three more are still protected because of their authors' death dates: Cortot (died 1962), Dohnányi (died 1960) and Philipp (died 1958), along with Burnam (died 2007).

### Cited Findings
- United States rule: works published before 1978 have a 95-year term; works from 1930 "were copyrighted for 95 years — through 2025 — and are in the public domain January 1, 2026" — [Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain](https://cspd.law.duke.edu/publicdomainday/2026/)
- "All published works from 1930 and before ... are in the public domain"; works published 1931–1963 kept copyright only if renewed in their 28th year — [Wikipedia, Public domain in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_domain_in_the_United_States)

## 5. What do current adult method books put first as a daily routine, and in what order do they introduce technique?

### Takeaway
Faber's adult course is the only one whose sequence could be read page by page: it attaches a "3-Minute Technique" page to every unit, starting with seconds and thirds for each hand alone in a C five-finger position, then broken thirds, parallel and contrary motion between the hands, rhythm drills, pentascales in other keys, wider intervals, and only then the one-octave C major scale, the G7 and primary chords, and the G scale; Book 2 adds pedalling, the F scale, triads and inversions, Alberti bass, minor scales, and one-octave arpeggios last. Alfred's adult course follows the same broad order (five-finger positions, then scales and chords key by key) and adds isometric hand exercises and finger-strengthening drills; Bastien's ends Book 1 with scales, chords and inversions.

### Cited Findings

#### Daily-routine advice from the exercise books themselves
- Hanon: play the exercises already learned at least once a day; the whole book takes an hour — [Schirmer text](https://archive.org/details/hanonvirtuosopia00hano)
- Burnam's subtitle and the publisher's advice: a dozen short exercises "to be done each day before practicing", learned two or three at a time — [Internet Archive record](https://archive.org/details/dozendaytechnica0000burn), [Hal Leonard](https://www.halleonard.com/product/404073)
- Burgmüller as daily material: keep three or four études in rotation for a week or so, then change — [Practising the Piano Online Academy](https://www.informance.biz/online-academy/page/132-introduction)
- Dohnányi, as one reviewer uses it: about 30 minutes, ten minutes on each of the three parts, varying the choice daily and resting when tired — [review article](https://classicalmusicpaolo.substack.com/p/essential-finger-exercises-by-ernst)

### Inferences
- The order all three adult methods share, read off the cited contents: (1) each hand alone in a five-finger position, steps then skips; (2) hands together in parallel and contrary motion; (3) five-finger positions in new keys; (4) wider intervals; (5) one-octave scale with thumb crossing in C, then G, then F; (6) the tonic chord, then V7, then IV, as blocked "close position" progressions; (7) pedalling; (8) triads and inversions, broken-chord and Alberti figures; (9) minor keys; (10) one-octave arpeggios; (11) faster note values. Faber supplies every step; Alfred and Bastien confirm steps 1, 3, 5 and 6.
- Faber's routine is short by design ("3-minute"), tied to the unit's new concept, and nearly always gives each hand alone before hands together; that is a usable template for a late beginner's daily warm-up in Prima.
- Arpeggios come last in the adult methods (Faber Book 2 Unit 15), whereas ABRSM asks for them at Grade 1; chords and cadences come early in the methods but are absent from ABRSM. A learner following ABRSM alone would miss the chord work the methods and RCM treat as basic.
- The methods put physical habits (posture, hand shape, fingertips, wrist release, arm weight) before note patterns; the nineteenth-century books do the reverse. An app can only judge the notes, so the habits would have to be given as written cues.

- Bastien: the chapter list rests on a Kjos listing seen only as a snippet (kjos.com refused access); nothing was found on a daily technique routine in the book.
